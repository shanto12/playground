The idea

the District at golden hour

Mood, story and who it is for.

Curry District as a neighborhood you can walk into: painted shop signs, marigold garlands, truck-art color, and a steaming bowl rising like the sun through a bazaar gate.

Moodboard in words

  • A marigold ceiling you walk under at the door.
  • A hand-painted “district street sign” by a local sign painter.
  • A truck-art box that reads from across the counter, with a pun on the lid.
  • A chai cup with a sticker-bold shadow and a tilted, marker-script tag.
  • One hero dish people line up to photograph.

Best for

  • Younger families
  • Delivery-app regulars
  • Reels & stories
  • Party trays
  • The lunch rush

Voice

one Hindi phrase per screen

Six sample lines, written from the copy deck for this direction.

Playful, loud-and-proud, food-first. Short punchy lines, District street-sign energy and a light sprinkle of Hindi or Hinglish, one phrase per screen at most.

  • Welcome to the District. Bring your appetite.Website hero
  • Butter chicken that deserves its own zip code.Menu & social
  • Bas, ek aur naan. Just one more.Napkin & bagHindi: “okay, just one more naan.”
  • Every street leads to swaad.TaglineSwaad = taste, flavor.
  • Andhra fire. Tandoor smoke. Wok-tossed swagger.Window poster
  • Pick your heat. We’ll bring the swaad.Ordering screen

Do

  • Name real dishes; specifics beat adjectives.
  • Use the District map: quarters, the boulevard, the alley.
  • Gloss a Hindi phrase in English the first time it appears.

Don’t

  • Claim halal, vegan, gluten-free, “authentic” or awards.
  • Use “exotic” or turban-and-elephant clichés.
  • Invent scarcity (“selling out”, “today only”).

The idea

candlelight, brass and jewel tones

Mood, story and who it is for.

Curry District as a modern palace dining room: jewel tones, brass and candlelight, a Mughal arch framing a handi of slow-simmered curry, and saffron-gold foil on midnight.

Moodboard in words

  • An aubergine box with one saffron-gold foil print and lots of empty space.
  • A single arch and jali screen, backlit, over a pink or emerald banquette.
  • Marble, brass katoris and candlelight gleaming on the metal.
  • Fraunces italic headlines in foil against deep plum.
  • A tiny ritual: a brass tray of saunf at the end of the meal.

Best for

  • Date nights
  • Family celebrations
  • Catering & events
  • Upscale-casual
  • Higher average ticket

The voice

sentences that smell like cardamom

Six sample lines, written from the copy deck for this direction.

Warm, confident, sensory. Unhurried sentences, specific dishes and gracious hospitality. Premium but never stiff: a jewel-toned dining room that still feels like the neighborhood.

  • Slow-simmered. Tandoor-fired. Made for sharing.Tagline
  • Dinner, made an occasion.Website hero
  • The lid lifts. The evening begins.Biryani reel
  • Gather close. The good plates are coming.Menu cover
  • Generous plates. Gracious welcome.Window & door
  • Every celebration deserves a table like this.Catering page

Do

  • Lead with the senses: char, steam, butter, tang, smoke.
  • Sound like a gracious host: “we” and “you”, never “patrons”.
  • Let one word per headline carry the gold foil.

Don’t

  • Claim halal, vegan, gluten-free, “authentic” or awards.
  • Reach for palace clichés: maharajas, elephants, “fit for a king”.
  • Put Hindi in Royal headlines; keep it to the Bazaar voice.

Recipe 1 · Royal leads, Bazaar colors it

Royal’s logo, type and arches; Bazaar’s patterns, brights and playful lines. Friendly-premium.
