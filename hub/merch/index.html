04

Take-home jars: check first

The chili-oil and masala-tin labels are design concepts. Everything legal on them is a placeholder.

Food labeling and Texas cottage-food / health-department rules must be checked before anything is sold. The labels show [ingredients], Net wt [__] and [blend name] as placeholders, with no nutrition, health or origin claims.

  • Texas cottage-food rules are written for home kitchens. A restaurant selling packaged jars may instead fall under its local food permit or need a state food-manufacturer license. Ask the Town of Little Elm food-permits office and Texas DSHS.
  • Typical US label items to confirm: product name, net quantity, ingredient list, allergen statement, and the business name and address.
  • Chili oil made with fresh garlic or aromatics needs a food-safety process review before it goes on a shelf.

How to avoid a closet full of unsold tees

  1. Uniforms in bulk, once. Order to headcount plus about 20% spares in core sizes. It’s known demand.
  2. Customer merch on print-on-demand. A QR on the table tent opens the shop. Nothing is made until someone buys.
  3. Graduate the winners. Move a design to a bulk run only after it has clearly sold through POD.
  4. Drops by pre-order. Open a short window for a seasonal capsule, then print exactly what was ordered.
  5. Pins and stickers as rewards. They’re cheap enough to hand out, which turns them into word of mouth.
