  1. 01

    Idea 01 of 10 · Phase 1

    Brand refresh & logo

    One confident look, everywhere you show up.

    CoversLogo, colours & type, plus uniforms and merch

    Effort: Medium Impact: High Cost $–$$ approx First win: ≈ 2 wks (logo + sticker)

    The problem today

    Three different “about us” blurbs, two emails and a Frisco-vs-Little Elm name split, so nothing ties the look together.

    Source: Presence audit · listings verified, brand score 3/10 (medium confidence)

    The idea

    Pick one of two directions, loud and joyful Bazaar or jewel-toned Royal, and we build a full kit: wordmark, emblem, colours, type, pattern and a spice scale. Then the team wears it: aprons, tees and caps.

    What you get

    • Wordmark in 6 versions (primary, stacked, emblem, one-colour, reversed…)
    • One-page colour, type & pattern guide
    • 5-step chili spice icons + veg / non-veg marks
    • Staff apron, tee & cap designs
    • Fan sticker pack & tote
    • Print-ready files for any printer

    See it

    Open the brand bookUniforms & merch

    Why it works sourced

    Sweetgreen’s refresh rolled one system across store, signage, uniforms, packaging and app, so the brand feels inevitable wherever you meet it.

  3. 03

    Idea 03 of 10 · Phase 1–2

    Takeout packaging system

    Every bag rides home as a little billboard.

    CoversBags, boxes, cups, seal stickers & stuffer cards

    Effort: Low Impact: High Cost $–$$ approx First win: ≈ 2 wks (seal sticker)

    The problem today

    Delivery apps hold your biggest audience, 2,000+ ratings, and those guests meet you through the bag, not the dining room.

    Source: Review research · delivery-app volume (medium confidence)

    The idea

    One ownable pattern and colour band on every bag, box, lid and cup, sealed with a “District stamp” sticker that carries a QR to order direct. Start with the sticker and a stuffer card; printed bags follow.

    What you get

    • 2-inch bag-seal sticker with QR
    • Bag-stuffer card: spice guide + reheat tip
    • “Rice included ✓” checklist sticker
    • Printed handled bag & clamshell label
    • Chai cup + sleeve
    • Party-tray lid label

    See it

    Open the packaging samples

    Why it works sourced

    Chipotle printed two-minute stories on cups and bags across 1,600 restaurants, which turned the bag into something people kept and read.

  5. 05

    Idea 05 of 10 · Phase 2

    Menu & table design

    Menu “districts”, a spice scale, easy first picks.

    CoversMenus, table tents & placemats

    Effort: Low Impact: Medium–High Cost $ approx First win: 1–2 wks (table tents)

    The problem today

    A broad menu (North Indian, Andhra & Hyderabadi, tandoor, Indo-Chinese), and reviews disagree on how hot “Mild” really is.

    Source: Presence audit (likely) · review research on spice (low–medium)

    The idea

    Group the menu into eight “districts” (Biryani Boulevard, Tandoor Quarter…), add an honest five-step chili scale, and lead with proven favourites such as biryani, butter chicken and garlic naan. No prices are printed until you confirm them.

    What you get

    • Single-page menu, front & back
    • 8 menu “districts” with icons
    • 5-step spice scale (“Mild still has a kick”)
    • Fan-favourites block, no invented prices
    • QR table tent: full menu + “tell us how we did”
    • District-map placemat for first-timers

    See it

    Open the menu samples

    Why it works sourced

    Wingstop’s named, colour-coded flavour set turns the menu itself into the brand.

  6. 06

    Idea 06 of 10 · Phase 1

    Google Business & photo makeover

    Where most hungry people meet you first.

    CoversCover, 25–30 photos, menu, posts & a review QR

    Effort: Low Impact: High Cost $ approx First win: a weekend

    The problem today

    The photos people find on Yelp and Restaurantji look mostly like guest snapshots, and your Tripadvisor listing shows zero reviews.

    Source: Presence audit · Tripadvisor likely, photo quality low confidence

    The idea

    One photo day with the real dishes, then a rebuilt Google profile: new cover, 25–30 tagged photos, the full menu, weekly posts, one main phone number, and a review QR that asks every guest equally, with no incentives.

    What you get

    • Photo-day shot list (25 shots)
    • Cover & logo sized for Google
    • Description + categories, checked with you
    • 4 weekly post templates
    • Review replies for 1–5 stars
    • Policy-safe review QR card

    See it

    Open the Google makeover

    Why it works sourced

    Google’s own widely quoted figure: listings with photos get 42% more direction requests and 35% more website clicks than listings without. That compares photos with no photos; it is not a promise.

12

Open questions we need from you

We only used what we could find publicly. These answers make everything real, and nothing gets printed until they’re in.

  1. Your story

    Who started Curry District, and why? We couldn’t find the founder or chef story anywhere online.

  2. Current logo files

    Vector files (AI, EPS, SVG or PDF) or the sharpest image you have, so the refresh starts from what guests already know.

  3. One phone number

    We found three different numbers across listings. Which one should every sign, bag and listing show?

  4. Real hours

    Yelp’s hours differ from other listings, and a weekday happy hour shows up once. What is true today?

  5. Spice levels

    How many heat levels do you offer, and how hot is “Mild” in your kitchen? This shapes the spice scale.

  6. Halal & alcohol facts

    Is any meat halal? Listings say BYOB; is that right? We print no halal, vegan or alcohol claims until you confirm.

  7. Photo day

    Which day suits a 3–4 hour shoot, and which 10 dishes are your heroes?

  8. Landlord sign criteria

    Can your property manager share the centre’s sign rules? They decide what the storefront can carry.

  9. Little Elm or Frisco?

    Is “Little Elm (near Frisco)” right for you, and is @currydistrictfrisco your Instagram account?

  10. Catering, loyalty & apps

    What catering do you offer today, is there any loyalty program, and which delivery apps are active?
