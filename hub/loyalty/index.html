Sample 08 · Loyalty, catering & print

Make every visit count

A stamp card that sends people through all eight districts of the menu, party-tray sheets that turn one dinner into a catering order, and neighborhood print that walks right up to the front door.

A · Bazaar B · Royal 24 print pieces + 1 demo

01

Where independents win repeat business

The big chains run points apps. A neighborhood kitchen can do something warmer, and cheaper.

Repeat visits

A stamp card people actually finish

Chains turn a QR scan into loyalty points, and the lesson from the slower programs is that the first win has to come quickly. The Curry Passport gives one stamp per menu district, so every visit gets a little closer to a full card.

From the chains playbook

Bigger tickets

Party trays as a real product

No local rival appears to market named, branded party trays, even with a strong celebration culture nearby. Here, the eight districts become tray categories, with write-in sizes and no prices until the owners set them.

Hypothesis: confirm by a quick scroll of rivals

Getting found

Paper that reaches the door

Door hangers, a 6 × 9 postcard and an event table kit are made for HOAs, schools, offices and festival nights: the places a Little Elm family already gathers.

Local tie-ins below

02

The Curry Passport

A 3.5 × 2 in wallet card. Order from a district, get its stamp. Collect all eight.

Rubber-stamp postmarks in eight festival inks. The reward line stays a placeholder for the owners.

Arch seals in jewel-tone inks; gold can be printed or foiled.

  1. Order from a districtAny dish from Starters Square to Chai & Coolers counts.
  2. Get its stampEight 1 in self-inking stamps live at the counter.
  3. Fill all eightThe reward is the owners' call. Nothing is promised on the card.

03

Try the digital stamp card

Tap a district to stamp it. It lands with a thud and a burst of marigold petals (Bazaar) or gold embers (Royal).

  • Progress is saved on this phone only.
  • Show this at the counter opens a full-screen card with a live clock and a moving shine, so staff can tell it isn't a screenshot.
  • Flip between Bazaar and Royal inside the demo.
  • Reduced-motion friendly: no particles, no shake.

Concept demo, no real rewards. A live version would plug into the ordering or POS system the restaurant already uses (Clover online ordering is listed publicly). Check what it supports before promising anything.
