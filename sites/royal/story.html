Chapter III

Kitchen vocabulary

A few words you will meet on our menu, explained in plain English.

DDumthe sealed pot
Slow cooking in a covered, sealed pot, so rice and meat steam gently in their own aromas. It is how a dum biryani is finished.On the menu: Chicken Dum Biryani, Goat Dum Biryani
TTandoorthe clay oven
A tall clay oven fired very hot. Marinated meats and paneer roast on skewers inside it, and naan is pressed onto its walls to puff and blister.On the menu: Tandoori Chicken, Garlic Naan
TTadkathe tempering
Whole spices, garlic or curry leaves sizzled in hot oil or ghee, then poured over a dish such as dal for a last burst of aroma.On the menu: Dal Thadaka
MMasalathe spice mix
Simply “a mix of spices”, ground or whole. It can mean a dry blend or the spiced gravy a dish is cooked in.On the menu: Paneer Tikka Masala, Channa Masala
GGongurasorrel leaves
Sorrel leaves with a sharp, tangy sourness, a favorite in Andhra-style cooking. They bring brightness to rich rice and meat dishes.On the menu: Gongura Mutton Pulav, Gongura Veg Biryani
KKarampodispice powder
A coarse, fiery spice powder of dried red chilies and spices. “Karam” means heat and “podi” means powder.On the menu: Karampodi Chicken
