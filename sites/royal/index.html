Chapter III

Tandoor & Handi

Two kinds of heat shape much of our menu: the fierce, dry fire of a clay oven, and the gentle, sealed steam of dum.

Fire, clay and smoke

The tandoor

A tandoor is a deep clay oven, fired until its walls radiate fierce heat. Marinated chicken and paneer go in on long skewers and take on a smoky char at the edges. Naan is pressed straight onto the hot clay wall, where it puffs and blisters in moments.

  • Clay oven
  • Long skewers
  • Char & blister
Enter the Tandoor Quarter
Sealed, steamed, unhurried

The handi, on dum

Dum means breath. A deep, round-bellied pot called a handi is layered with rice and spiced, marinated meat or vegetables, then sealed, often with a rope of dough, and left over gentle heat. The steam stays inside, so every grain drinks in the spice below.

  • Layered
  • Sealed
  • Slow-steamed
