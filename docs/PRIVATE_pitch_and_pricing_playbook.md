## 2. Three proposal tiers (all prices are **EST**, adjust to your costs and market)

À la carte sums below use the table above. Bundles run about 15–25% under the à la carte total.

### Spark (about 2–3 weeks), EST $1,500–2,800
- Google Business Profile audit and refresh (categories, hours check, description, photo curation, Q&A seed).
- 2-hour mini photo shoot with 15–20 edited images (on-site, shot in the closed afternoon window).
- One-page menu or takeout flyer with a QR code.
- Mini brand sheet (1 page: colours, fonts, logo lockups) in one of the two directions (Bazaar or Royal).
- 6 post templates and 3 story templates.
- 1 revision round per item.

### Signature (about 6–8 weeks), EST $6,000–10,000, recommended anchor
- Everything in Spark, plus the logo refresh and lockups.
- 12–15 page brand guidelines.
- Brochure site on a single page set that links to the Clover ordering page.
- Full dine-in plus takeout menu redesign.
- 2 packaging SKUs (bag seal sticker, box or tray sticker).
- 20-template social kit.
- Half-day photo shoot (about 25–30 images).
- 2 revision rounds. Optional retainer.

### Full District (about 10–14 weeks), EST $12,000–22,000
- Everything in Signature.
- 6-SKU packaging system (bag, box, sauce cup, napkin band, delivery sticker).
- Art direction for 2–3 walls, wallpaper or signage. This is design files only. A vendor installs.
- Motion kit (logo sting, 6 reels, 12 animated story templates).
- Full-day shoot (30–40 images).
- Catering page and menu updates for 90 days.
- 3 revision rounds.

### Optional retainer, "Keep it Glowing", EST $350–900/month
- 8–12 posts and 2–4 reels per month.
- Weekly Google Business Profile posts.
- Festival and seasonal promos.
- Menu tweaks and a one-page monthly report. Month-to-month with 30 days' notice.

### Commercial terms (not legal advice; have a lawyer review your template)
- **Payment.**
  - Spark: 50% deposit, 50% before final files.
  - Signature and Full: 40% deposit, 30% at design approval, 30% on handover.
  - Net 7 days. The deposit is non-refundable once work starts.
  - Files are released on full payment.
- **Revisions.** Rounds are as listed per tier. Extra rounds are billed at **EST** $75–125/hour. Feedback comes from one decision-maker, consolidated into one list.
- **Ownership [memory].** In the US the creator owns copyright until it is assigned in writing. Use a clause along these lines:
  - *"On receipt of full payment, Designer assigns to Client copyright in the final approved deliverables. Designer keeps rights in unused concepts and working files, and may show completed work in a portfolio with Client's written consent. Third-party fonts, stock and software are licensed separately."*
  - Prefer assignment over "work for hire". Work-for-hire status for a commissioned logo is shaky unless the work fits a statutory category [memory].
  - Photos: licence them to the restaurant for all its channels, including delivery apps. Reserve extended or resale rights, since photo sources note that extended commercial rights add cost.
- **Client supplies.**
  - Existing logo and photo files.
  - Verified menu text and prices. The client is responsible for allergen and dietary claims, and you never invent them.
  - Admin access to Google Business Profile and social accounts via invites, never passwords.
  - Floor-plan or wall measurements.
  - Landlord or permit approval for signage and murals. I did not research Little Elm sign rules or lease limits.
  - A reply within 48 hours.
- **Who pays suppliers.** The restaurant pays printers, installers, muralists and stylists directly, or prepays you 100% with a 0–15% handling fee. You never front print costs.

## 3. Approaching the owner

**When.** Go Tuesday to Thursday, about 2:45–3:45 PM, in the closed gap between lunch and dinner (Mon–Thu 2:30–4:30 per `business.json`, **unverified**). Confirm the hours on Google first. Avoid Friday to Sunday, lunch, and from 5 PM on. Better still, eat lunch there at 1:45 PM on a weekday. Tip well, ask for the owner, and say you will return at 3 PM.

**Phone kit.**
- Charged phone with brightness up.
- Hub link, a QR code to it, and an offline copy of the key screens in case the Wi-Fi is bad.
- A 20-second silent reel (mute-friendly).
- Your own screenshots of their public Google Business photo grid, shot at the visit. Do not mock them.
- Concept samples: sticker, menu, wallpaper.
- A business card and a one-page leave-behind.

**5-minute pitch script.**
1. **Hook (20 seconds).** "I ate your butter chicken last week. It tastes better than your photos look. Can I show you 60 seconds of what that could look like?"
2. **Their pain (45 seconds).** Describe only what you have observed: photos that do not match the food, a website and socials that feel generic, and orders going through delivery apps. State the commission tier only if you have confirmed it.
3. **Show (90 seconds).** Hold the phone. Show the sticker on a takeout bag, the menu, the wall, then the reel. Say: "Both directions are free concepts. Pick one."
4. **Proof (45 seconds).** Use your own portfolio, and add a placeholder for it if you have none. Add their public ratings only after you verify them (`business.json` lists Google 4.2, Facebook 4.8 and Yelp 4.0 on 120 reviews, all unverified). Do not invent testimonials.
5. **Offer (60 seconds).** A free 15-minute Google Business Profile audit, then the pilot below.
6. **Next step (20 seconds).** "Can I come back Wednesday at 3 with the audit?" Book a specific time.

**Objections.**
- *We're busy.* "That's why I'm here at 3. It costs you 30 minutes in week 1 and I do the rest."
- *No budget.* Give the Spark tier or the pilot, then the break-even maths below.
- *We have a website.* "Yes, and your Clover ordering works. I'm only making the front door match the food."
- *My nephew does our Facebook.* "Keep him. I'll give him a template kit so his posts look consistent."
- *How do I know it works?* Baseline Google Business Profile Insights and Clover and delivery order counts before starting, and review at day 90.
- *Who owns it?* They do, after final payment.
- *I need to ask my partner.* Leave the one-pager and set a callback time.

**Low-risk first offers (EST).**
1. Free 15-minute Google Business Profile audit with screenshots. This costs you time only.
2. **Paid pilot, $450–900.** One takeout sticker, a one-page menu or table tent, and a 25–50 piece proof print. Turnaround 7–10 days. Credit 100% toward Signature if they sign within 30 days.
3. Half-day mini photo shoot, $450–750 (Austin-type half-days run $350–1,000 per the photo sources).
4. Avoid giving away a free shoot. The concept work is already your free giveaway.

**ROI framing: use their numbers, not mine.** Ask for last month's direct and delivery-app order counts and average ticket. The sums below are **illustrative arithmetic, not research**.
- If the average ticket is $30 (placeholder) and the app commission is 25% (**[memory]**: DoorDash and Uber Eats publish tiers of roughly 15%, 25% and 30%; confirm on their current merchant pages), each app order costs $7.50.
- A direct order costs about 3% in card processing, around $0.90 (**[memory]**, rough).
- The saving is about $6.60 per order shifted to direct. A $2,000 project breaks even after about 300 shifted orders, or about 25 a month for a year.
- Photo impact: I recall Google-attributed claims that profiles with photos get more direction and website requests (**[memory]**, old, vendor-repeated, not verified). Do not quote percentages. Show their own Google Business Profile Insights instead.
- Repeat-order uplift: the often-cited "5% retention gives 25–95% profit" (Bain/Reichheld, **[memory]**) is cross-industry and contested. Do not use it for a single-location restaurant.

## 4. Etiquette and legal (not legal advice)

- **It is unsolicited spec work.** Frame it as a proposal. Professional design bodies generally discourage spec work **[memory]**, so be upfront that they owe nothing.
- **Disclaimer.** Keep the footer from `AGENT_RULES.md`: *"Independent design concept prepared as a proposal — not the official Curry District website."* Add: *"Curry District and any related names or marks belong to their owners and appear only to illustrate this proposal. No endorsement implied."*
- **Keep `noindex,nofollow` on every page.** Share only the private link, not social posts, groups or ads. Do not buy lookalike domains.
- **Their marks.** Use the name and facts only for the pitch. No scraped logos or photos. Keep the concept visibly distinct from any real Curry District mark.
- **Written permission first.** Get an email or text approval before any public launch, social post, portfolio listing, or use of their name in marketing.
- **If they say no.** Thank them. Within 48 hours, take down the hub and sites (remove the Netlify deploy) and delete any portfolio mentions. Confirm in writing and do not re-pitch for 6 months unless they ask.
- **Have your contract template lawyer-reviewed once.**

## 5. 30/60/90-day rollout after a yes

**Days 0–30**
- Sign and collect the deposit (days 0–3).
- 45-minute kickoff on site, and the client picks Bazaar or Royal by day 7.
- Capture baseline metrics: Google Business Profile Insights, Clover orders, delivery-app share, Instagram followers.
- Google Business Profile refresh goes live by day 14.
- Photo shoot in the closed afternoon window (week 2).
- Menu v1 and sticker proofs.
- First 6 posts live.

**Days 31–60**
- Brand guidelines final.
- Site built on a noindex staging link and launched only after written approval.
- The restaurant orders menus and stickers directly from the printer.
- Template kit handed to whoever posts (the nephew included), plus a 30-minute staff walkthrough.
- First 1–2 reels.
- QR codes on bags that point to direct ordering.

**Days 61–90**
- For the Full tier: wall and signage art direction goes to vendors, and quotes and landlord approval come in. Installation is scheduled after hours, which sources say adds about 50% to mural labour.
- Compare results with the day-0 baseline.
- Day-90 review meeting.
- Propose the retainer.
- Ask permission for a portfolio case study and testimonial.
- Hand over the source-file folder, and invoice the final milestone.
